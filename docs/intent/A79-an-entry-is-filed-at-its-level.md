# A79 An entry is filed at the level it is, and its source is recorded apart

A goal is why the project exists, and no piece of the work completes it. An objective is a change seen from outside that names no mechanism. An action is the mechanism. The owner can say any of the three, so a request of the owner's is filed at its own level under the goal it serves, with `source: owner` and the quote. When no goal fits, the session asks the owner why they want it, and the answer becomes the goal. Only the owner states a goal, as before.

## Context

The rule read "a goal is the owner's words", and the process gave a goal's writer in concrete terms and its nature only as "an abstract, high-level need". Sessions filed by source: gallery reached seventeen goals in three days, eleven of them single requests copied into a goal file, and the objectives beneath them also named the real goal. The rule that an untraced action is not taken pushed the same way, since quoting the owner's message closed the gap without asking. The owner saw it in gallery and approved the definitions on 2026-10-27. The reading is `docs/analyses/intent-levels.md`.

## Options

- Keep the rule and repair each record by hand. Serves nothing: the next request lands as a goal again.
- Define each level by what it says, with a test per level, and separate the source. Taken.
- A gate on the shape of the ladder, such as a goal with a single objective. Rejected as a gate: the zero-warning bar would fail every young project, whose first goal has one objective. A listing of request-shaped goals in `INDEX.md` is left open.

## Consequences

The rulebook grows by about sixty words. A session files a request below a goal, which needs it to read the active goals first, and asks one question when none fits. The misfiled goals already in gallery, `mat-lib`, `ow-as-python`, `abstract-refactor` and `generic-app` stay until each project repairs them, by retiring each with the entry it now is. Undone if sessions file real needs of the owner's as objectives and the goals stop saying why the project exists.
